## Week 1: What Change Navigation Is
Most of the stress around AI is not about the technology. It is about the feeling that things are moving faster than we can process.

Change navigation is the skill of staying oriented when everything is shifting. It is not a personality trait. It is not reserved for technical people. It can be learned. And it is becoming the most underrated professional skill of the next decade.

The women who thrive in the AI era will not be the ones who learned to code at 14. They will be the ones who know how to assess, decide, adapt, and move forward without freezing.

## Week 2: AI Is Not the Disruption
The headline says AI will replace workers. The quieter truth is that AI will replace workers who do not use AI.

This is not a threat. It is a recalibration. The skills that become more valuable — judgment, perspective, the ability to read a situation — are exactly the skills that experience builds and AI cannot replicate.

Learning to use AI is not about becoming technical. It is about making sure your non-technical skills have the right amplifier.

The question that stumps you at 10 PM is the same question three other people had that week. In a group, someone asks out loud and everyone learns. Alone, you close the laptop.

## Week 7: What AI Cannot Replace
AI can produce text. It cannot know what text needs to be written. It can summarize a meeting. It cannot sense the tension in the room that the summary omits.

These are not technical limitations. They are category differences. AI processes information. Humans interpret meaning.

The skill to develop is not learning more about AI. It is learning more about the boundary between what AI does and what you do. The sharper that boundary, the clearer your value.

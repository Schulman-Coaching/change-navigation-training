Part 3: The Change Navigation Framework

Change follows a pattern. When you learn the pattern, you stop reacting and start navigating. This framework works for any transition — career change, new technology, family shift, personal growth.

1
See — Name What Is Changing

Before you can navigate change, you have to see it clearly. Most people skip this step. They feel the discomfort of change and move immediately to action — any action — to relieve the pressure.

Ask yourself: What exactly is changing? Is it a tool, a role, a relationship, an expectation? What is the scope? Who else is affected? What am I assuming that might not be true?

Write it down. Naming the change reduces its power to disorient you. A named thing is a thing you can work with.

2
Assess — What Is Actually at Stake

Not everything that feels urgent is important. Not everything that feels threatening is a threat. Separate the signal from the noise.

Ask yourself: What is the worst that could realistically happen? What is the best? What is most likely? What am I afraid of losing? What might I gain? Whose voice am I hearing when I evaluate this — mine, or someone else's?

Most fear lives in the gap between "what if" and "what is." Close the gap by writing down facts versus assumptions.

3
Decide — Choose a Direction

You will never have complete information. Waiting for certainty is waiting forever. The skill is deciding with what you have.

Ask yourself: What is the smallest decision I can make right now that moves me forward? What is the cost of waiting? What is the cost of being wrong? Which decision aligns with who I want to become?

Make the decision. Write it down. Tell someone. A decision that is not committed to paper or shared with another person is still a wish.

4
Act — Move, Observe, Adjust

Action produces information that thinking cannot. You learn more from one attempt than from ten hours of planning. The goal is not to get it right the first time. The goal is to start the feedback loop.

Ask yourself: What is the smallest action I can take today? What will I learn from it? How will I know if it is working? What is my rhythm for reviewing and adjusting?

Act. Observe the result. Adjust. Repeat. This loop is the engine of change navigation.

Part 4: Worksheets

Four guided exercises. One for each core skill. Do them in order or start with the one that feels most relevant right now.

Worksheet 1: Clarity — Mapping Your Change Landscape

Name the changes you are navigating. Get them out of your head and onto the page.

1. What is the biggest change you are navigating right now?
2. What about this change feels uncertain or uncomfortable?
3. What assumptions am I making about this change that might not be true?
4. If I were advising a friend in this situation, what would I tell her?
5. What is one thing I know for sure about this situation?

Worksheet 2: Confidence — Building Your Change Muscle

Confidence comes from doing. Track the actions that build it.

1. What is something I did in the past that I did not think I could do?
2. What did I learn from that experience about my own capability?
3. What is a small challenge I can take on this week — something slightly outside my comfort zone?
4. Who in my life believes in me more than I believe in myself right now?
5. What would I attempt if I knew I could not fail?

Worksheet 3: Adaptability — The Learning Loop

Adaptability is not a trait. It is a practice: act, observe, adjust, repeat.

1. Think of a recent situation where something did not go as planned. What happened?
2. What did you learn from that experience?
3. How quickly did you adjust? What helped you pivot? What slowed you down?
4. What is one area of your life where you have become more adaptable over time?
5. What is one new skill or tool you could learn this month that would increase your adaptability?

Worksheet 4: Agency — From Reactive to Proactive

Agency is the belief that you have a say in what happens next. It is built one decision at a time.

1. When do I feel like change is happening TO me rather than something I am choosing?
2. What is one area of my life where I have more control than I usually acknowledge?
3. What decision have I been postponing? What is the cost of continuing to postpone it?
4. If I made that decision today, what would the first step look like?
5. What is one thing I will decide this week — and act on — to reclaim a sense of direction?
